North Peak B1 Massif

A first authored replacement for the North Peak macro-terrain, not another correction of the R1 mountain. The design is a derived design master that the terrain is generated from. B1.1: SIGNIFICANT ARTISTIC PROGRESS · NOT YET ART-APPROVED

This is the first candidate that reads as a mountain massif rather than a modified smooth hill. The smooth R1 dome is gone. In its place is an asymmetric crown: a dominant horn summit (897 m), a deep notch, a lower Tarntind to the south-east and a Vesttind shoulder to the west. The arms differ, and from the north the lee side is cut by two deep, dark cirque bowls under clean arêtes. Seen in real Three.js renders under both clay presets, the skyline from Birchwood moves up to 34 px, against 4 px for A1c, and changes character, not just height.

It is not yet art-approved, for three reasons. (1) From the two identity views (Birchwood, Central Valley shoulder) the new crown sits on a long, smooth plinth that is mostly R1 terrain outside the study area. The mountain still reads as a crown on a hill rather than one monumental mass. (2) The ridge flanks come from an analytic flank profile, and in raking light some faces show planar, triangular facets with straight edges (south-west oblique). The surface reads as sculpted clay rather than eroded rock. (3) The authored Western Col (668 m) came out at 729 m, so Vesttind is only a shoulder. Two authored arm ends also meet the frozen coast band 50–66 m too high, which creates short artificial sea-cliff steps (2.1 ha and 1.7 ha).

Per the brief, one corrective iteration was made after the first renders (B1.0 → B1.1). It removed the crumpled-paper texture and the extruded look of the arms, and gave North Peak clear dominance. No further sculpting was done.

3 · Artistic evaluation

Judged on the real captures under both sun presets, not on displacement or pixel counts.

Does it finally look like a compelling Nordic mountain massif?Partly yes. From the north, south-east and south-west obliques, and from the plateau, it reads as one glaciated massif with a horn, arêtes, cirques and buttresses. From the far identity views it reads as a convincing summit crown on a smooth hill. The lower two thirds of that view are outside the study area.
Silhouette characterMuch stronger: three unequal tops with a deep notch next to the summit replace a single dome. North Peak dominates after the correction. Tarntind no longer competes from Birchwood.
Geological coherenceGood at macro scale: the lee cirques face north and east, a broad windward fjell lies to the south-west, arms radiate from one summit, and drainage follows the new troughs. Weak at the meso scale: there are no rock bands, steps or talus, and the faces are smooth analytic planes.
AsymmetryYes: the west arm is broad and gentle, the north arm a narrow arête and the south-east arm stepped. The windward and lee sides are clearly different from the north and south-west.
Natural ridge connectionsArms and buttresses connect to the summit and to each other; nothing is a disconnected patch. One weak connection is the Western Col, which is filled.
Convincing glacial erosionConvincing from the north, where two cirque bowls read clearly. From the south the hanging cirque is visible only as a soft hollow, and the troughs have no stepped floors or lips yet.
Procedural repetitionNo repeated cones or parallel scratches. The risk that remains is planar facets from the flank function in raking light.
Spatial depth from player heightBetter: from the Vestfjell plateau and the route shoulder the summit rises as a horn behind real foreground relief. From Birchwood and the Central Valley the depth is limited by the unchanged plinth.
Hiking appealThe candidate route climbs the windward fjell and a south-west shoulder to the summit with views into the lee cirques. It is walkable end to end; the last 140 m are a declared steep summit scramble.
Consistency across directionsConsistent: the same massif reads from every direction, and each side has its own character.

7 · Local design-authority changes

  • New derived design master tools/geo-r02c-b01/design/north-massif-b1.json (revision B1.1; B1.0 kept in docs/geo-r02c-b01/iteration/) supersedes the A-R1 North massif ridges n-main (west of x 260), n-crown, n-west-spur (north of z 1900), n-nw-cape (east of x −1330) and n-east-arc (west of x 430) inside the study area only. The A-R1 master itself is unchanged.
  • Summit hierarchy re-authored: North Peak ≈ 900 m kept at its approved position; new Tarntind (768), Vesttind (724), Nordhorn (738) and Austtind (700); saddles N1–N4.
  • Crest sections lowered below their R1 levels (Skar notch −98 m, North gap −102 m) and secondary summits raised above R1 (Tarntind +81 m, Vesttind +100 m).
  • Four cirques and outlet troughs carved (down to −195 m); the lowland envelope lowered inside the massif core and tapered to zero at the coast, the south and east edges.
  • Drainage regenerated inside the study area: 17.6 ha change destination; High Tarn catchment 102.3 → 95.3 ha. Lake, level, shore and inlet channels are untouched.
  • Peak-ascent realigned (candidate) from where it enters the edited terrain; the R1 alignment is kept as reference. The candidate includes a declared summit scramble above the 0.30 grade target.

Limitations

  • Clay renders only; no vegetation, rock material, snow or atmosphere.
  • The study area excludes the long southern plinth seen from Birchwood and the Central Valley, so the identity views change only in their upper part.
  • Heightfield on a 4 m grid: vertical cliffs and overhangs are not possible, and arêtes are limited to about 8 m width after smoothing.
  • Two conditioned cirque/trough hollows (0.4 ha, 6.6 m) are dry tarn sites: the water surface is not regenerated in B1.
  • Boundary seam: where the west arm and the Vesttind north buttress meet the 70 m coast freeze, B1 is 50–66 m above R1, so the slope steepens to about 2.5 over 2.1 + 1.7 ha. The fix is to end those arm levels before the coast band.
  • The Vestfjell camera was placed on the B1.0 route; the corrected B1.1 route passes 132 m to the east.

Recommendation

Keep B1.1 as the new baseline direction. The method (an authored design master generating the regional heightfield, with explicit boundary conditions and regenerated drainage) works, and the forms finally read from the player viewpoints. Do not return to corrective overlays on R1.

Smallest practical route to an art-approved North Peak, in order. (1) Extend the study area south over the plinth below the massif, to about z 1300, so the identity views gain the foreground shoulders and valley head. This needs a separate authorization, because it reaches the Cairn approaches. (2) Add one meso-scale layer: rock bands and steps on the headwalls, stepped trough floors with lips, and talus aprons, driven by slope and the authored structures, not noise. (3) Fix the two coast-band seams and open the Western Col. (4) Regenerate the water surface for two or three cirque tarns. Then repeat one independent art review on the same cameras.
